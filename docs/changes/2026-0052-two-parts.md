# 2026-0052. Two parts: DBIx::Squirrel, then Ekorn::Pipeline

**Status:** open

**Decisions:** D1 settled: Part 1 is DBIx::Squirrel, complete; Part 2

**Origin:** roadmap, added 2026-11-26

## Background

DBIx::Squirrel's documents teach two ways of getting results, side by
side: its own iterator (`next`, `first`, `single`, `all`,
`all_arrayref`), and a pipeline that ends in one of Ekorn::Pipeline's
sinks (`keep_all`, `keep_first`, `to_file` and the rest). The guide's
table under "8. Sinks" sets `->all` beside `keep_all` as two ways to
get every result. The one put first is the one DBIx::Squirrel doesn't
own. On 2026-11-26, `keep_all` appeared 5 times in `README.pod`, where
the iterator's `->all` appeared twice; 29 times in `docs/HOWTO.md`;
and 25 times in `docs/REFERENCE.md`. The guide's "The layers, at a
glance" ends its diagram in a sink, and "Import everything from one
place" comes before its first chapter.

So the documents tell DBIx::Squirrel's story in Ekorn::Pipeline's
words, and a reader has to learn both to follow either. 2026-0051,
listing Tooling's 25 names from Ekorn in the reference's index, made
that plainer, and Iain saw in it the coupling between the two
distributions that the split was meant to escape.

Talking it over on 2026-11-26, Iain and Claude settled on what
DBIx::Squirrel is: the DBI, minus the boilerplate, with a pipeline of
stages beside the query, complete in itself. A reader takes as much as
they need and stops when they want. Ekorn::Pipeline brings most of the
concepts, and taking it on is a separate choice, not a step every
reader must make. DBIx::Squirrel is where a reader who wants those
concepts can start, with their own queries as the first examples. The
aim is to show synergy, not dependency: how the two, once split, can
be put back together to take DBIx::Squirrel much further.

So each document comes in two parts. Part 1 covers DBIx::Squirrel in
full. Part 2, brief, says where Ekorn::Pipeline fits, and how to build
on it for work with databases. Why the two split is told there
(2026-0053). Once the documents are split, what Part 1 still takes from
Ekorn::Pipeline's code shows plainly, and can be cut (2026-0057).

The need driving the change is to be explained, not left implicit, as
Iain asked on 2026-11-26: here, and in `Changes`, where each item's
entry says why the documents changed, the coupling they showed and
the synergy they aim for, as well as what moved.

This item settles where the line falls, for all three documents, and
how it's kept. 2026-0054, 2026-0055 and 2026-0056 split each document
by it.

## Decisions

- [x] **D1.** What goes in each part? Part 1 is DBIx::Squirrel, in
  full: a reader who stops at its end has a complete library. Part 2 is
  brief: where Ekorn::Pipeline fits into the story, and how to build on
  it for work with databases. Settled by Iain on 2026-11-26: the
  documents should convey not dependency but synergy, and the split
  shows how the two can be put back together to extend DBIx::Squirrel
  much further.
- [ ] **D2.** Which names are Ekorn's, and so kept out of Part 1? The
  recommendation: any name a caller imports from an Ekorn::Pipeline
  module, which takes in Tooling's 25 (2026-0051), the sinks among
  them, and Ekorn::Pipeline's own name. Part 1 then says what comes
  back with the iterator's methods. Stages, Context, `Stop`, `gather`
  and the rest of DBIx::Squirrel's own modules stay in Part 1, though
  they run on Ekorn::Pipeline underneath: a reader never needs to know
  that. Still to settle within it: whether DBIx::Squirrel::Tooling
  belongs in Part 1 for its own tags, `:rows` and `:context`, or moves
  to Part 2 whole.
- [ ] **D3.** How are the parts marked, and numbered? The
  recommendation: a `##` heading for each part, the same words in all
  three documents, such as "Part 1: DBIx::Squirrel" and "Part 2: With
  Ekorn::Pipeline", the chapters numbered on through both, so a link
  to a chapter changes only where the chapter moves. `scripts/manuals`
  and the anchors `xt/reference-index.t` checks are to be tried with
  it before it's settled.
- [ ] **D4.** Does Part 1 say what it asks a reader to learn, and end
  where they may stop? The recommendation: yes. It opens by naming its
  concepts (placeholders, the iterator, stages), and ends by saying the
  reader has a complete library, and what Part 2 adds for those who
  want more. Part 2 opens by naming what Ekorn::Pipeline adds (sources,
  sinks, `feed`, `tee`). A reader who can see the cost can choose to
  stop, and isn't dropped into it.
- [ ] **D5.** How is the line kept? The recommendation: an author test,
  beside `xt/examples.t`, failing where a name of Ekorn's (D2) appears
  in a document's Part 1, in its code or its text, and checking only
  documents that have a Part 2 heading, so it can come with the first
  document split (2026-0054) and take in the others as they're split.
