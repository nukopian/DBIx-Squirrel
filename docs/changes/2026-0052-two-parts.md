## Background

DBIx::Squirrel's documents teach two ways of getting results, side by
side: its own iterator (`next`, `first`, `single`, `all`,
`all_arrayref`), and a pipeline that ends in one of Ekorn::Pipeline's
sinks (`keep_all`, `keep_first`, `to_file` and the rest). The guide's
table under "8. Sinks" sets `->all` beside `keep_all` as two ways to
get every result. The one put first is the one DBIx::Squirrel doesn't
own. On 2026-11-26, `keep_all` appeared 5 times in `README.pod`, where
the iterator's `->all` appeared twice; 29 times in `docs/HOWTO.md`;
and 25 times in `docs/REFERENCE.md`. The guide's "The layers, at a
glance" ends its diagram in a sink, and "Import everything from one
place" comes before its first chapter.

So the documents tell DBIx::Squirrel's story in Ekorn::Pipeline's
words, and a reader has to learn both to follow either. 2026-0051,
listing Tooling's 25 names from Ekorn in the reference's index, made
that plainer, and Iain saw in it the coupling between the two
distributions that the split was meant to escape.

Talking it over on 2026-11-26, Iain and Claude settled on what
DBIx::Squirrel is: the DBI, minus the boilerplate, with a pipeline of
stages beside the query, complete in itself. A reader takes as much as
they need and stops when they want. Ekorn::Pipeline brings most of the
concepts, and taking it on is a separate choice, not a step every
reader must make. DBIx::Squirrel is where a reader who wants those
concepts can start, with their own queries as the first examples. The
aim is to show synergy, not dependency: how the two, once split, can
be put back together to take DBIx::Squirrel much further.

So each document comes in two parts. Part 1 covers DBIx::Squirrel in
full. Part 2, brief, says where Ekorn::Pipeline fits, and how to build
on it for work with databases. Why the two split is told there
(2026-0053). Once the documents are split, what Part 1 still takes from
Ekorn::Pipeline's code shows plainly, and can be cut (2026-0057).

The need driving the change is to be explained, not left implicit, as
Iain asked on 2026-11-26: here, and in `Changes`, where each item's
entry says why the documents changed, the coupling they showed and
the synergy they aim for, as well as what moved.

This item settles where the line falls, for all three documents, how
it's kept, and where the new documents are written. 2026-0054,
2026-0055 and 2026-0056 write each document by it, in `docs/next/`.

## Decisions

- [x] **D1.** What goes in each part? Part 1 is DBIx::Squirrel, in
  full: a reader who stops at its end has a complete library. Part 2 is
  brief: where Ekorn::Pipeline fits into the story, and how to build on
  it for work with databases. Settled by Iain on 2026-11-26: the
  documents should convey not dependency but synergy, and the split
  shows how the two can be put back together to extend DBIx::Squirrel
  much further.
- [x] **D2.** Which names are Ekorn's, and so kept out of Part 1? Any
  name a caller imports from an Ekorn::Pipeline module, which takes in
  Tooling's 25 (2026-0051), the sinks among them, and Ekorn::Pipeline's
  own name; and DBIx::Squirrel::Tooling goes to Part 2 whole, as Iain
  decided on 2026-11-26. Part 1 says what comes back with the
  iterator's methods, and imports from DBIx::Squirrel's own modules.
  Stages, Context, `Stop`, `gather` and the rest of those modules stay
  in Part 1, though they run on Ekorn::Pipeline underneath: a reader
  never needs to know that. Tooling's work is importing everything
  from one place, Ekorn's names with it, so it's a convenience for
  Part 2. Showing it in Part 1 for its own tags, `:rows` and
  `:context`, was the alternative, but it would put Tooling in both
  parts.

  Amended by Iain on 2026-11-26, after GitHub Copilot's review of the
  1.6.5 evidence (2026-0053): what goes in Part 1 has a positive test
  as well. Does it fit naturally in 1.6.5's DESCRIPTION, or extend
  something it names? That DESCRIPTION is SQL kept close, placeholders
  made less bothersome, iterators and transformations for results, and
  everything the DBI does still done. The review proposed the first
  half; Claude added the second, as `Stop`, `gather`, Context, missing
  values caught, and lists into `IN (...)` came after 1.x, but each
  extends transformations, iterators or placeholders, and belongs in
  Part 1. Sinks, files, JSON, CSV, `feed` and `tee` fail it, being
  about data crossing the database's edge, not the round trip to it,
  and go to Part 2. The test of names (D5) stays the gate, mechanical
  and never drifting; this one guides judgement as the documents are
  written, where names can't decide, such as an idea that names
  nothing of Ekorn's but reads as its way of seeing things.
- [x] **D3.** How are the parts marked, and numbered? A `##` heading
  dividing the parts, with a short introduction under it, the same
  words in all three documents, "Part 1: DBIx::Squirrel" and "Part 2:
  With Ekorn::Pipeline"; the chapters keep their own `##` headings,
  numbered on through both parts, as Iain decided on 2026-11-26.
  Heading levels, the POD and most anchors stay as they are. Chapters
  nested a level under each part, or Part 2 as a document of its own
  beside each, were the alternatives: the first changes every
  chapter's anchor, the index's links and the POD's heading levels;
  the second makes more files and more links between them.
- [x] **D4.** Does Part 1 say what it asks a reader to learn, and end
  where they may stop? Yes, as Iain decided on 2026-11-26. It opens by
  naming its concepts (placeholders, the iterator, stages), and ends by
  saying the reader has a complete library, and what Part 2 adds for
  those who want more. Part 2 opens by naming what Ekorn::Pipeline adds
  (sources, sinks, `feed`, `tee`). A reader who can see the cost can
  choose to stop, and isn't dropped into it.
- [x] **D5.** How is the line kept? An author test, beside
  `xt/examples.t`, failing where a name of Ekorn's (D2) appears in a
  document's Part 1, in its code or its text, as Iain decided on
  2026-11-26. It checks only documents that have a Part 2 heading, so
  it comes with the first document written (2026-0054) and takes in
  the others as they're written.

  Amended by Iain on 2026-11-26, after the baseline: what the test
  looks for is the `Ekorn::` namespace, in a module's name, and the
  names imported from it (D2); not the word "ekorn" itself. The
  attributes prefixed `ekorn_`, such as `ekorn_slice`, are
  DBIx::Squirrel's own, as its attributes have always been, and belong
  in Part 1. So no example in Part 1 uses the `Ekorn::` namespace.
- [x] **D6.** How are today's documents kept while the new ones are
  written? Untouched, as Iain suggested on 2026-11-26, as source and
  style material that may prove important, and still what's
  published. The new README, guide and reference are written in
  `docs/next/`, with their tests; one commit then puts them in place
  of the old (2026-0058), once all three are done. Rewriting on an
  experimental branch, or in place with a copy of the old to one side,
  were the alternatives: the first can't show the two side by side in
  one tree, and the second changes the published documents as the
  work goes.
- [x] **D7.** How is success measured? Four ways, as Iain decided on
  2026-11-26, the last the one that counts, the others evidence for it:
  1. **Counted, and kept by a test.** No name of Ekorn's in any
     document's Part 1, as the D5 test checks. Today's baseline is in
     Background: `keep_all` 5 times in the README, where `->all` is
     twice.
  2. **A cold reader.** A fresh agent, given only Part 1, does real
     work: connecting, a query with placeholders, rows shaped by
     stages, results collected; it passes if it needs none of
     Ekorn::Pipeline's words. Given Part 2 as well, it does work that
     needs it, such as a query's results written to a JSON file, or a
     CSV loaded in batches, showing whether Part 2 extends Part 1 or
     rescues it. It's run on today's documents first, before any is
     rewritten, for a baseline, and on the new ones before the swap
     (2026-0058).
  3. **Coupling, before and after,** for 2026-0057: the checks of ours
     that a change in Ekorn::Pipeline can break (today
     `xt/ekorn-tooling.t`, and the anchors `xt/reference-index.t`
     checks), the releases of ours an Ekorn release forced, and the
     messages between the two sessions for each release.
  4. **Iain's judgement,** whether Part 2, opening with why the two
     split, reads as synergy. He reviews the documents on their own
     merits with GitHub Copilot, as a sceptical reader, telling it
     what is intended and why.

  The README and the guide carry the sales pitch: what DBIx::Squirrel
  is worth to a reader, and what Ekorn::Pipeline adds. It must appeal
  to sceptical and enthusiastic readers alike, and the new documents
  are judged on that, not only on where the line falls.

  Amended by Iain on 2026-11-26, after a first sceptical review of the
  plan, by GitHub Copilot:
  - The cold reader's "real work" is, given Part 1 alone: queries with
    placeholders; a large result set read as a stream; rows
    transformed; inserts, updates and deletes; and one row fetched, or
    many. Part 1 passes if the reader never feels blocked.
  - The D5 test is the negative form of the aim; the positive form is
    that a reader can learn Ekorn::Pipeline exists only after Part 1.
    As D2 counts Ekorn::Pipeline's own name among Ekorn's names, the
    test checks both.
  - Coupling before and after (3) is evidence after the documents, not
    a goal of them: the documents show the coupling, and 2026-0057's
    work in the code reduces it.
  - Every claim of a gain ("simpler", "less boilerplate", "more
    flexible") is followed at once by code that shows it. The README
    and the guide show it in pairs, plain DBI beside DBIx::Squirrel,
    for work a DBI user does every week, plain DBI being the baseline;
    a large result set can use the gazetteer data in the test
    databases. The examples' database shows how things work; the pairs
    show why they're worth having.
- [x] **D8.** What's the smallest description of DBIx::Squirrel that
  stays true if Ekorn::Pipeline disappeared tomorrow? This, as Iain
  decided on 2026-11-26:

  > You write the SQL; DBIx::Squirrel takes away the DBI machinery
  > around it, with placeholders in any common style, a list into
  > `IN (...)` from one placeholder, and results read through an
  > iterator, and every handle is still a DBI handle. Rows can also be
  > changed as they're read, by small functions written beside the
  > query.

  It's Part 1's foundation, and replaces "the DBI, minus the
  boilerplate, plus a pipeline" in the guide's opening, the README
  and, in time, `CLAUDE.md`. Every word of it holds without
  Ekorn::Pipeline. It leads with control of SQL, the strongest
  difference from an ORM, and says every handle is still a DBI handle,
  for readers wary of wrappers.

  It came from the review's sharpest question. Claude's first try
  listed five gains in one sentence, missing values caught among them,
  and stages as "rows shaped by small functions". GitHub Copilot's
  review of it, as a sceptical reader, found each clause true and
  easily shown beside plain DBI but the last, which no opening line
  can sell: a reader who has never wanted stages needs an example, so
  the description only introduces them, in a sentence of their own,
  and the stages chapter makes the case. Missing values caught moved
  out of the opening, to follow it at once with an example. The review
  also noted that it remaining DBI was missing, and is what wary
  readers most need to hear. Its own wording was corrected where it
  claimed more than the documents can show: a list's expansion isn't
  automatic, but asked for with a placeholder written `...`; and
  "fully compatible with DBI" became every handle still being a DBI
  handle, which the tests show. Its hedge, "removes much of", went, as
  the pairs show what goes.

  Stages pass the review's test, that they're defended without naming
  Ekorn: they were 1.x's transformations, before Ekorn::Pipeline
  existed (2026-0053), so transforming rows beside the query is
  DBIx::Squirrel's own idea, as are its iterators. So the rewrite
  restores ownership of DBIx::Squirrel's own ideas; it doesn't hide an
  outside influence. Owning stages doesn't settle when they're
  taught: they come where they're useful, after rows are read.

  It also gives the parts a line a reader can see: Part 1 is the round
  trip to the database, query in and rows out; Part 2 is data crossing
  the database's edge, rows to files, JSON and CSV, loaded from them in
  batches, or sent to several places in one run. So Part 2 is a
  different job, not the better half held back, which the review
  named as the structure's biggest risk.

## The plan

**What:** `docs/next/` made ready for the new README, guide and
reference (D6), with the tests that read `docs/` reading it too; and
the cold reader's baseline (D7), taken from today's documents before
any is rewritten.

**Why:** 2026-0054 to 2026-0056 write the new documents there, and
each draft should be tested as it's written, not only at the swap
(2026-0058). The baseline is what the new documents are measured
against, and can only be taken before they replace the old.

**Steps:**

1. `docs/next/README.md`, saying what the directory is for, and that
   it goes at the swap. Nothing else reads it: `docs/` is out of the
   distribution, and `scripts/manuals` reads only `docs/HOWTO.md` and
   `docs/REFERENCE.md`.
2. `xt/examples.t` runs the examples in `docs/next/HOWTO.md` and
   `docs/next/REFERENCE.md`, where they exist, as it runs `docs/`'s,
   and compiles those in `docs/next/README.pod`, as it does
   `README.pod`'s.
3. `xt/spelling.t` converts `docs/next/`'s Markdown to POD with
   `scripts/manuals`' own conversion, as the manuals will be made at
   the swap, and checks its spelling, and `docs/next/README.pod`'s. So
   a draft's construct the conversion refuses, or a word it doesn't
   know, fails on the push that brings it.
4. Each test tried with a sample document in `docs/next/`: an example
   whose comment is wrong, a misspelt word, and a construct the
   conversion refuses each make it fail. The samples aren't committed.
5. The cold reader's baseline. A fresh agent, given today's README,
   guide and reference, copied apart from the repository and nothing
   else, writes programs for D7's tasks against an SQLite database:
   - Part 1's: a query with placeholders; a large result set, 100,000
     rows, read as a stream; rows transformed as they're read; an
     insert, an update and a delete; one row fetched, and many.
   - Part 2's: a query's results written to a JSON file; a CSV file
     loaded into a table in batches.

   Each program is run, and recorded: whether it works; the names of
   Ekorn::Pipeline's it uses, by D2; and where the reader says it was
   unsure or blocked. Today's documents have no Part 1, so the baseline
   gives the reader all three; with the new ones, it's given Part 1
   alone for Part 1's tasks.
6. `ROADMAP.md` and this record: what happened, and the baseline.

**Done when:** `xt/examples.t` and `xt/spelling.t` read `docs/next/`
as they read `docs/`, and fail on each sample of step 4; `yath test -L
xt` passes on 5.36 and 5.44; perlcritic and perltidy are clean; and
the baseline is in this record.

## What happened

- **Outcome:** done. `docs/next/README.md` says what the directory is
  for. `xt/examples.t` runs the examples in `docs/next/HOWTO.md` and
  `docs/next/REFERENCE.md`, and compiles those in
  `docs/next/README.pod`, where they exist. `xt/spelling.t` converts
  `docs/next/`'s Markdown with `scripts/manuals`' conversion and checks
  its spelling, and `docs/next/README.pod`'s. With sample documents
  in `docs/next/`, not committed, each test passed on good ones, and
  failed, naming the file, on a wrong comment, a misspelt word in the
  Markdown and in the README, a level-five heading the conversion
  refuses, and a README example that doesn't compile. `yath test -L t
  xt` passed on 5.36 and 5.44 against Ekorn-Pipeline's working copy at
  446058f8873907be8d53f2befd49bbaa70e6f101; perltidy is clean.
- **Changes from the plan:** `xt/spelling.t` checks `lib`'s POD a file
  at a time, as `all_pod_files_spelling_ok` sets a plan of its own,
  and ends with `done_testing`. A misspelling in the Markdown is
  reported at its line in the POD made from it, not the Markdown's,
  with the word, which is enough to find it. Perlcritic, which CI runs
  on `lib` alone, flags `die` and backticks in the two tests, as it
  did before in `xt/examples.t`; the new lines follow that file's
  pattern.
- **The baseline (D7):** a fresh agent, given today's README, guide
  and reference, copied apart from the repository and nothing else,
  wrote and ran the seven programs. All seven worked, six at the first
  attempt.
  - **Part 1's tasks** needed three concepts, the reader said: the
    placeholder styles; `iterate` with `next`, `single` and `all`; and
    a stage as a sub giving none, one or several values. Their
    programs used no name of Ekorn's by D2: `drop_if`, though imported
    from Tooling, is exported by DBIx::Squirrel::Stages::Flow too.
  - **Part 2's tasks** needed `to_json`, `from_csv`, `batch`,
    `Ekorn::Pipeline->new` and its `iterator`, with sinks and Tooling's
    tags: "a newcomer still has to learn two libraries' names to do a
    file load", and the CSV recipe's `Ekorn::Pipeline->new(...)
    ->iterator(...)` is "a different mental model from
    `$dbh->iterate`".
  - **Gaps:** where `\%attr` goes among `iterate`'s bind values (its
    one failed attempt; only the reference's index gives the order);
    which module exports `drop_if`; `do` giving `0E0` when nothing
    changes; `to_json` never shown in the guide, nor what its settings
    are; and `iterator` on a pipeline never explained.
  - **What read as another library's:** the README brings in sinks,
    words, the Sugar, `gather`, `|` and `@{}`, `pipe` and `tap`, feeds,
    `result_original` and Ekorn::Pipeline before its sixth step;
    Tooling's table has 13 tags from about 10 modules, "two
    distributions' worth"; and attributes named `ekorn_`, such as
    `ekorn_slice`, "are a surprise in a DBIx::Squirrel module". That
    prefix is DBIx::Squirrel's own, as Iain said, so it stays in Part
    1 (D5, amended); the surprise is one for Part 1 to explain.
  - **What the reader took DBIx::Squirrel to be:** a DBI subclass with
    flexible placeholders and lists, checked bind values, a lazy
    iterator over results, and stages attached to a query: close to
    D8.

  So today's documents let a cold reader do Part 1's work; the cost is
  the vocabulary they meet on the way, most of it Ekorn::Pipeline's,
  and the gaps above. The new documents are measured against this.
- **What's left:** the gaps, for 2026-0054 and 2026-0055 to close.
