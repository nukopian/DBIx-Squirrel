**Status:** open

**Origin:** roadmap, added 2026-11-26

## Background

DBIx::Squirrel's documents teach two ways of getting results, side by
side: its own iterator (`next`, `first`, `single`, `all`,
`all_arrayref`), and a pipeline that ends in one of Ekorn::Pipeline's
sinks (`keep_all`, `keep_first`, `to_file` and the rest). The guide's
table under "8. Sinks" sets `->all` beside `keep_all` as two ways to
get every result. The one put first is the one DBIx::Squirrel doesn't
own. On 2026-11-26, `keep_all` appeared 5 times in `README.pod`, where
the iterator's `->all` appeared twice; 29 times in `docs/HOWTO.md`;
and 25 times in `docs/REFERENCE.md`. The guide's "The layers, at a
glance" ends its diagram in a sink, and "Import everything from one
place" comes before its first chapter.

So the documents tell DBIx::Squirrel's story in Ekorn::Pipeline's
words, and a reader has to learn both to follow either. 2026-0051,
listing Tooling's 25 names from Ekorn in the reference's index, made
that plainer, and Iain saw in it the coupling between the two
distributions that the split was meant to escape.

Talking it over on 2026-11-26, Iain and Claude settled on what
DBIx::Squirrel is: the DBI, minus the boilerplate, with a pipeline of
stages beside the query, complete in itself. A reader takes as much as
they need and stops when they want. Ekorn::Pipeline brings most of the
concepts, and taking it on is a separate choice, not a step every
reader must make. DBIx::Squirrel is where a reader who wants those
concepts can start, with their own queries as the first examples. The
aim is to show synergy, not dependency: how the two, once split, can
be put back together to take DBIx::Squirrel much further.

So each document comes in two parts. Part 1 covers DBIx::Squirrel in
full. Part 2, brief, says where Ekorn::Pipeline fits, and how to build
on it for work with databases. Why the two split is told there
(2026-0053). Once the documents are split, what Part 1 still takes from
Ekorn::Pipeline's code shows plainly, and can be cut (2026-0057).

The need driving the change is to be explained, not left implicit, as
Iain asked on 2026-11-26: here, and in `Changes`, where each item's
entry says why the documents changed, the coupling they showed and
the synergy they aim for, as well as what moved.

This item settles where the line falls, for all three documents, how
it's kept, and where the new documents are written. 2026-0054,
2026-0055 and 2026-0056 write each document by it, in `docs/next/`.

## Decisions

- [x] **D1.** What goes in each part? Part 1 is DBIx::Squirrel, in
  full: a reader who stops at its end has a complete library. Part 2 is
  brief: where Ekorn::Pipeline fits into the story, and how to build on
  it for work with databases. Settled by Iain on 2026-11-26: the
  documents should convey not dependency but synergy, and the split
  shows how the two can be put back together to extend DBIx::Squirrel
  much further.
- [x] **D2.** Which names are Ekorn's, and so kept out of Part 1? Any
  name a caller imports from an Ekorn::Pipeline module, which takes in
  Tooling's 25 (2026-0051), the sinks among them, and Ekorn::Pipeline's
  own name; and DBIx::Squirrel::Tooling goes to Part 2 whole, as Iain
  decided on 2026-11-26. Part 1 says what comes back with the
  iterator's methods, and imports from DBIx::Squirrel's own modules.
  Stages, Context, `Stop`, `gather` and the rest of those modules stay
  in Part 1, though they run on Ekorn::Pipeline underneath: a reader
  never needs to know that. Tooling's work is importing everything
  from one place, Ekorn's names with it, so it's a convenience for
  Part 2. Showing it in Part 1 for its own tags, `:rows` and
  `:context`, was the alternative, but it would put Tooling in both
  parts.
- [x] **D3.** How are the parts marked, and numbered? A `##` heading
  dividing the parts, with a short introduction under it, the same
  words in all three documents, "Part 1: DBIx::Squirrel" and "Part 2:
  With Ekorn::Pipeline"; the chapters keep their own `##` headings,
  numbered on through both parts, as Iain decided on 2026-11-26.
  Heading levels, the POD and most anchors stay as they are. Chapters
  nested a level under each part, or Part 2 as a document of its own
  beside each, were the alternatives: the first changes every
  chapter's anchor, the index's links and the POD's heading levels;
  the second makes more files and more links between them.
- [x] **D4.** Does Part 1 say what it asks a reader to learn, and end
  where they may stop? Yes, as Iain decided on 2026-11-26. It opens by
  naming its concepts (placeholders, the iterator, stages), and ends by
  saying the reader has a complete library, and what Part 2 adds for
  those who want more. Part 2 opens by naming what Ekorn::Pipeline adds
  (sources, sinks, `feed`, `tee`). A reader who can see the cost can
  choose to stop, and isn't dropped into it.
- [x] **D5.** How is the line kept? An author test, beside
  `xt/examples.t`, failing where a name of Ekorn's (D2) appears in a
  document's Part 1, in its code or its text, as Iain decided on
  2026-11-26. It checks only documents that have a Part 2 heading, so
  it comes with the first document written (2026-0054) and takes in
  the others as they're written.
- [x] **D6.** How are today's documents kept while the new ones are
  written? Untouched, as Iain suggested on 2026-11-26, as source and
  style material that may prove important, and still what's
  published. The new README, guide and reference are written in
  `docs/next/`, with their tests; one commit then puts them in place
  of the old (2026-0058), once all three are done. Rewriting on an
  experimental branch, or in place with a copy of the old to one side,
  were the alternatives: the first can't show the two side by side in
  one tree, and the second changes the published documents as the
  work goes.
- [x] **D7.** How is success measured? Four ways, as Iain decided on
  2026-11-26, the last the one that counts, the others evidence for it:
  1. **Counted, and kept by a test.** No name of Ekorn's in any
     document's Part 1, as the D5 test checks. Today's baseline is in
     Background: `keep_all` 5 times in the README, where `->all` is
     twice.
  2. **A cold reader.** A fresh agent, given only Part 1, does real
     work: connecting, a query with placeholders, rows shaped by
     stages, results collected; it passes if it needs none of
     Ekorn::Pipeline's words. Given Part 2 as well, it does work that
     needs it, such as a query's results written to a JSON file, or a
     CSV loaded in batches, showing whether Part 2 extends Part 1 or
     rescues it. It's run on today's documents first, before any is
     rewritten, for a baseline, and on the new ones before the swap
     (2026-0058).
  3. **Coupling, before and after,** for 2026-0057: the checks of ours
     that a change in Ekorn::Pipeline can break (today
     `xt/ekorn-tooling.t`, and the anchors `xt/reference-index.t`
     checks), the releases of ours an Ekorn release forced, and the
     messages between the two sessions for each release.
  4. **Iain's judgement,** whether Part 2, opening with why the two
     split, reads as synergy. He reviews the documents on their own
     merits with GitHub Copilot, as a sceptical reader, telling it
     what is intended and why.

  The README and the guide carry the sales pitch: what DBIx::Squirrel
  is worth to a reader, and what Ekorn::Pipeline adds. It must appeal
  to sceptical and enthusiastic readers alike, and the new documents
  are judged on that, not only on where the line falls.
